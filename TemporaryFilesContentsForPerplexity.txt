# Brand Direction — Corehouse Pilates Studio


This document presents 2–3 plausible aesthetic directions for the Pilates studio website, each aligned with the studio's positioning as premium, trustworthy, and focused on precision and wellness.


**Purpose**: Choose one direction as the foundation for design tokens and visual system implementation.


**Constraints**: Avoid generic SaaS aesthetics, purple gradients, template-like three-column grids, and placeholder imagery. Prefer restrained color, strong typography, clear hierarchy, and authentic content.


---


## Direction 1: Refined Minimalist


### Mood


- **Keywords**: Serene, sophisticated, authoritative, understated luxury
- **Tone**: Calm yet confident; speaks to precision and control
- **Impression**: High-end wellness studio with mastery of craft; minimal distraction, maximum focus
- **Best for**: Studios targeting professionals, serious practitioners, and those seeking premium refinement


### Typography


- **Headings**: Clean sans-serif, geometric (e.g., Inter, Grotesk, or similar)
  - Large, spacious, minimal weight variation (regular/bold)
  - Generous leading for calm, open feel
  - Example: H1 in 48–56px with 1.1–1.2 line-height
- **Body**: Same sans-serif or slightly warmer companion (e.g., Figtree, Graphik)
  - 16–18px for comfortable reading
  - High contrast with ample letter-spacing
- **Accent**: Possibly a subtle serif for hero taglines or testimonials (optional, used sparingly)


### Color Direction


- **Primary palette**:
  - Off-white/cream base (`#fafaf8` or similar) — clean, not pure white
  - Deep charcoal/near-black (`#1a1a1a`) — for text and strong elements
  - Subtle warm accent (taupe, warm gray, or soft sage `#8b8680` or `#7a9b8e`) — for secondary CTAs, accents, borders
- **Secondary**:
  - Pale cream for surfaces (`#f5f3f0`)
  - Soft shadow/divider in warm gray (`#ebe8e3`)
- **Accent** (if needed):
  - Minimal use of warm terracotta (`#c9856f`) or soft bronze (`#a89968`) — for key CTAs only
- **Overall**: 85% cool/warm neutrals; 15% accent. Low saturation; high contrast for legibility


### Imagery Direction


- **Photography style**: Clean, well-lit studio spaces; close-ups of movement and posture; natural light
- **Subjects**: Diverse age ranges and body types; authentic practitioners in mid-movement; focus on precision and control
- **Color in photos**: Natural, muted tones; avoid oversaturated colors or heavy post-processing
- **Illustration** (if used): Minimalist line art; anatomical diagrams of form; simple geometric abstraction of movement
- **Avoid**: Generic stock photos, models in perfect lighting, overly stylized or heavily filtered imagery


### Spacing and Layout


- **Density**: Very open and spacious; generous margins and padding
- **Grid**: 6–12 column grid; favor 2–3 column layouts for content
- **Section spacing**: Sections separated by 80–120px vertical space
- **Container max-width**: 1000–1100px (narrower than typical to maintain focus)
- **Emphasis via whitespace**: Content hierarchy built on spacing as much as visual weight


### Motion and Interaction


- **Transitions**: Slow, gentle; 300–500ms ease-in-out for link hovers and state changes
- **Microinteractions**: Minimal; subtle scale or opacity change on hover
- **Scroll**: No fixed parallax or heavy animation; smooth scroll behavior
- **Load states**: Fade-in rather than slide-in; respect prefers-reduced-motion


### Why This Direction Fits Pilates


- Pilates is about **precision, control, and intentionality** — minimalism mirrors this philosophy
- The studio appeals to **professionals and practitioners** who value substance over flash
- **Calm environment** supports the wellness positioning
- **Clear hierarchy** reflects the structured, methodical nature of the practice
- **Spaciousness** echoes the feeling of a well-organized, uncluttered studio


### What to Avoid


- ❌ Heavy texture or busy patterns
- ❌ Warm earth tones only (risks looking aged/vintage)
- ❌ Colorful accent palette (diminishes focus)
- ❌ Playful or casual tone in copy or imagery
- ❌ Animation or motion that draws attention away from content
- ❌ Oversized hero sections or cinematic feel
- ❌ Multiple competing CTAs or visual anchors


### Reference Sites to Study


Study these manually for inspiration; do not copy directly:


1. **Reformer Pilates Studio (international)**: Look for how they balance professionalism with warmth; note their use of negative space and restrained color.
2. **Luxury wellness brands (e.g., premium yoga/wellness)**: Note clean typography, spacious layouts, and subtle use of earth tones.
3. **High-end personal training or coaching sites**: Study how they convey expertise and trust through calm, confident design.

## Direction 2: Warm Elegance


### Mood


- **Keywords**: Welcoming, warm, sophisticated, grounded, approachable
- **Tone**: Professional and expert, but not cold; inviting without being casual
- **Impression**: Upscale wellness community where precision meets warmth; you are cared for
- **Best for**: Studios targeting a broader audience; practitioners who value both expertise and personal connection


### Typography


- **Headings**: Serif typeface (e.g., Crimson Text, Lora, Volkhov, or similar)
  - 40–52px for H1; elegant, readable
  - Slightly higher serif weight than body for distinction
  - Conveys timelessness and sophistication
- **Body**: Warm sans-serif (e.g., Figtree, Poppins, or humanist sans)
  - 16–18px; friendly but not playful
  - Slightly rounded character shapes add warmth
- **Accent**: A secondary serif in smaller size for pull-quotes or testimonials (optional)


### Color Direction


- **Primary palette**:
  - Warm white/ivory base (`#fffaf5` or `#fef9f3`) — off-white with warmth
  - Deep warm brown or charcoal (`#2a2420` or `#3d3a35`) — text; warm rather than cool-black
  - Warm accent (soft terracotta, warm sage, or warm taupe `#c9a878`, `#8b9d89`, `#a89080`) — secondary CTAs, borders, subtle background tints
- **Secondary**:
  - Light cream or warm off-white for surfaces (`#f8f4f0`)
  - Warm divider/shadow (`#e8e2da`)
  - Soft warm accent for hover states (`#daa968` or `#b8a083`)
- **Overall**: 80% warm neutrals; 20% warm accents. Gentle saturation; readable but not harsh


### Imagery Direction


- **Photography style**: Warm, natural lighting (golden hour when possible); candid, relatable moments; focus on connection and form
- **Subjects**: Diverse practitioners; authentic smiles; close-ups of good form; instructors guiding students
- **Color in photos**: Warm light; natural skin tones; earthy, organic feel
- **Illustration** (if used): Watercolor or soft line art; organic, flowing lines that suggest movement; botanical or natural elements as accents
- **Avoid**: Cold, harsh lighting; overly polished or distant imagery; clinical feel


### Spacing and Layout


- **Density**: Open and spacious, but slightly more intentional rhythm than Refined Minimalist
- **Grid**: 8–12 column grid; allows for 3-column layouts with warmth
- **Section spacing**: Sections separated by 60–100px vertical space
- **Container max-width**: 1100–1200px
- **Emphasis**: Hierarchy through color, weight, and spacing; slightly more layering than minimalist approach


### Motion and Interaction


- **Transitions**: Smooth, moderate pace; 250–400ms ease-in-out
- **Microinteractions**: Gentle color shift or soft scale on hover; warmer accent color appears
- **Scroll**: Smooth scroll; minimal parallax; subtle color transitions as sections enter view (optional, light touch)
- **Load states**: Fade-in with slight color warmth; soft, welcoming feel


### Why This Direction Fits Pilates


- **Warm tone** suggests the personal, human side of the practice — connection with instructor and community
- **Elegance + warmth** conveys that the studio is both professional and welcoming
- **Serif typography** adds timelessness and craftsmanship, reflecting decades of Pilates tradition
- **Organic imagery and illustration** connects to the body-centered nature of the practice
- **Slightly richer color** allows for visual interest without chaos


### What to Avoid


- ❌ Overly warm or saturated tones (risks looking dated/rustic)
- ❌ Heavy serifs or very ornate typefaces
- ❌ Too many accent colors or competing visual elements
- ❌ Generic wellness clichés (candles, flowers, spa imagery)
- ❌ Copy that feels flowery or overly emotional
- ❌ Distracting animations or video backgrounds
- ❌ Layouts that feel cluttered despite warmth


### Reference Sites to Study


Study these manually for inspiration; do not copy directly:


1. **Upscale independent fitness/wellness studios**: Note how they balance warmth with authority; observe serif/sans pairings.
2. **Premium small-batch wellness brands**: Study how they use warm color and imagery to convey care without being commercial.
3. **Artisanal or craft-focused professional services**: Notice how they convey tradition and expertise through typography and restrained, warm color.

## Direction 3: Strong Modern


### Mood


- **Keywords**: Contemporary, empowering, confident, dynamic, grounded
- **Tone**: Progressive and professional; emphasizes strength and transformation
- **Impression**: Modern studio that understands both the science and the power of the practice; you are building strength
- **Best for**: Studios with younger practitioners or those seeking to emphasize the dynamic, transformative aspects of Pilates


### Typography


- **Headings**: Bold, contemporary sans-serif (e.g., Inter Bold, Source Sans Pro Bold, Dm Sans)
  - 44–56px; clean, direct, powerful
  - Slightly tighter letter-spacing for strength
  - Uppercase or mixed-case; both can work
- **Body**: Modern humanist sans (e.g., Figtree, Calibre, or similar)
  - 16–18px; open and readable
  - Friendly but purposeful
- **Accent**: Possibly a contrasting weight or subtle mono-width font for CTAs or labels


### Color Direction


- **Primary palette**:
  - Off-white/warm white base (`#faf9f7`)
  - Strong charcoal or dark navy (`#1f1f1f` or `#1a2332`) — for text; projects confidence
  - Modern accent (warm clay, warm sage, or modern bronze `#d4956d`, `#758b78`, `#a39079`) — secondary CTAs, accents, highlights
- **Secondary**:
  - Clean light background (`#f5f4f2`)
  - Warm gray divider (`#e8e5e0`)
  - Slightly bolder accent for active states (`#c9825c` or `#69765d`)
- **Overall**: 75% neutrals; 25% accent. Moderate saturation; confident without being loud


### Imagery Direction


- **Photography style**: Sharp, clean, well-lit; active movement captured mid-form; confidence and strength visible
- **Subjects**: Diverse practitioners; focused expressions; strong postures; dynamic mid-movement shots; transformation narratives
- **Color in photos**: Bright, clear lighting; good skin-tone rendering; perhaps subtle warm filter for warmth
- **Illustration** (if used): Geometric, dynamic line art; arrows or directional elements suggesting motion; abstract representations of strength and balance
- **Avoid**: Overly soft or dreamy imagery; still, posed-looking photos; clinical or clinical anatomical focus only


### Spacing and Layout


- **Density**: Purposeful spacing; not overly open, but clearly organized
- **Grid**: 12 column grid; allows for varied layouts (2, 3, 4 column) while maintaining rhythm
- **Section spacing**: Sections separated by 60–90px vertical space
- **Container max-width**: 1200–1280px (slightly wider; suggests modern web confidence)
- **Emphasis**: Clear hierarchy through color, weight, and directional flow; slightly more dynamic arrangement of elements


### Motion and Interaction


- **Transitions**: Crisp, purposeful; 200–350ms ease-in-out or ease-out for responsiveness
- **Microinteractions**: More noticeable hover effects; color shift to accent color; possible subtle scale
- **Scroll**: Smooth scroll; optional, light use of directional indicators (arrows, underlines appearing on scroll)
- **Load states**: Fade-in with slight upward motion (respectful, not jarring); conveys momentum


### Why This Direction Fits Pilates


- **Contemporary aesthetic** positions the studio as progressive and forward-thinking
- **Strong color and typography** align with the empowering, transformative nature of the practice
- **Dynamic layouts** suggest movement and energy without compromising the calming effect of Pilates
- **Modern imagery** attracts practitioners seeking both tradition and innovation
- **Confidence in design** reflects confidence in method and results


### What to Avoid


- ❌ Overly trendy design or colors that will feel dated soon
- ❌ All-caps headlines or design that shouts
- ❌ Heavy animation or effects that distract from content
- ❌ Overly geometric or abstract imagery that loses human connection
- ❌ Neon or very high-saturation accent colors
- ❌ Copy that sounds like aggressive fitness marketing
- ❌ Layouts that sacrifice clarity for visual novelty


### Reference Sites to Study


Study these manually for inspiration; do not copy directly:


1. **Contemporary independent fitness studios**: Note how they balance modernity with accessibility; observe their use of accent color and typography.
2. **Progressive wellness or health tech brands**: Study how they convey expertise and innovation simultaneously.
3. **Modern luxury brands** (non-fitness): Notice how they use bold typography and confident color choices without feeling chaotic.

## Decision Framework


Use these questions to choose the right direction:


1. **Who is the primary audience?**
   - Serious, premium-focused practitioners → Refined Minimalist
   - Mixed audience; community important → Warm Elegance
   - Younger practitioners or transformation-focused → Strong Modern


2. **What is the studio's positioning?**
   - Premium, exclusive, refined → Refined Minimalist
   - Accessible luxury, welcoming community → Warm Elegance
   - Modern, progressive, results-oriented → Strong Modern


3. **What feeling should people have visiting the site?**
   - Calm, focused, quiet confidence → Refined Minimalist
   - Warmth, connection, professional care → Warm Elegance
   - Energy, empowerment, dynamic strength → Strong Modern


4. **What visual treatment feels most authentic to the studio's actual culture and teaching style?**

## Naming conventions


### Test file naming


- **Collocated unit/integration tests**: `<Component>.test.tsx` next to `<Component>.tsx`
- **Utility test files**: `<module>.test.ts` next to `<module>.ts`
- **E2E test files**: `<feature>.spec.ts` under `e2e/` folder


### Test names (describe/it blocks)


Use clear, user-behavior-oriented test names:


```typescript
// ✅ Good
describe('SiteHeader', () => {
  it('renders logo and primary navigation')
  it('primary nav link has href matching route')
  it('header is keyboard navigable')
})


// ❌ Avoid
describe('SiteHeader', () => {
  it('renders')
  it('nav links work')
})
```

## Test file patterns


### Unit test template


```typescript
import { describe, it, expect } from 'vitest'
import { render, screen } from '@testing-library/react'


import Button from '@/components/ui/button'


describe('Button', () => {
  it('renders button with label', () => {
    render(<Button label="Click me" />)
    expect(screen.getByRole('button', { name: /click me/i })).toBeInTheDocument()
  })


  it('calls onClick when clicked', async () => {
    const handleClick = vi.fn()
    render(<Button label="Click" onClick={handleClick} />)
    await userEvent.click(screen.getByRole('button', { name: /click/i }))
    expect(handleClick).toHaveBeenCalledOnce()
  })
})
```


### Integration test template


```typescript
import { describe, it, expect } from 'vitest'
import { render, screen } from '@testing-library/react'


import Page from '@/app/(marketing)/page'


describe('Home Page', () => {
  it('renders page shell with main landmark', () => {
    render(<Page />)
    expect(screen.getByRole('main')).toBeInTheDocument()
  })


  it('renders primary heading', () => {
    render(<Page />)
    expect(screen.getByRole('heading', { level: 1 })).toBeInTheDocument()
  })
})
```


### E2E test template


```typescript
import { test, expect } from '@playwright/test'


test.describe('Navigation smoke tests', () => {
  test('home page loads and has main CTA', async ({ page }) => {
    await page.goto('/')
    expect(await page.status()).toBe(200)
    await expect(page.getByRole('heading', { level: 1 })).toBeVisible()
  })


  test('primary nav link to /about loads without 404', async ({ page }) => {
    await page.goto('/')
    await page.getByRole('link', { name: /about/i }).click()
    expect(page.url()).toContain('/about')
    expect(await page.status()).toBe(200)
  })
})
```


## Smoke-test philosophy


### What to test now (Milestone 2)


1. **Renderability**: components and pages render without errors
2. **Structure**: expected landmarks (`<main>`, `<nav>`, `<header>`, `<footer>`), headings, and layout nesting exist
3. **Navigation**: primary nav links are present and load destination pages
4. **Metadata**: robots, sitemap, and page titles are present
5. **Accessibility basics**: semantic HTML structure; keyboard nav on primary elements


### What to defer until later


- Full WCAG compliance scanning (axe-core integration)
- Form submission flows (requires backend/stub)
- Blog slug fixtures (requires sample content)
- Visual regression tests
- Full site link crawls
- Performance metrics


### Test count targets for Milestone 2


- **Unit tests**: 5–10 (layout components, placeholder, helpers)
- **Integration tests**: 5–8 (page rendering, nav composition)
- **E2E smoke tests**: 3–5 (critical user journeys, robots/sitemap checks)


Total: ~15–20 tests to establish baseline confidence.


## Selector guidance


### Prefer these selectors (in order of reliability)


1. **Role-based** (most resilient to UI changes)
   ```typescript
   screen.getByRole('button', { name: /submit/i })
   screen.getByRole('heading', { level: 1 })
   screen.getByRole('navigation')
   screen.getByRole('link', { name: /about/i })
   ```


2. **Label-based** (for form inputs)
   ```typescript
   screen.getByLabelText(/email/i)
   ```


3. **Text content** (for headings, labels)
   ```typescript
   screen.getByText(/about us/i)
   ```


4. **TestId** (when role/label not practical; use sparingly)
   ```typescript
   screen.getByTestId('hero-cta')
   ```


### Avoid these selectors (brittle; break easily)


- ❌ CSS classes: `.btn-primary`
- ❌ Element tags without context: `getByRole('button')` (too many)
- ❌ nth-child pseudo-selectors: `nth-child(2)`
- ❌ IDs that are not semantic: `#popup-123`


## Test boundaries


### Unit tests


**What**: Small, isolated components and utilities.


**Scope**: Render output, props handling, conditional logic, utility function behavior.


**NOT testing**: page routing, navigation between routes, API calls, complex interactions.


**Location**: Collocated with component or utility file.


**Examples**:
- `Button` renders with correct variant
- `PagePlaceholder` shows title and description
- `metadataHelpers.formatTitle()` returns expected shape


### Integration tests


**What**: Component composition, page structure, multi-component interactions.


**Scope**: Page layout, navigation presence, form composition, accessibility structure.


**NOT testing**: end-to-end user journeys, routing between different pages, external APIs.


**Location**: Collocated with page or feature.


**Examples**:
- Home page renders header, main content, footer
- Contact page renders form with expected fields
- Navigation component renders all expected links


### End-to-end tests


**What**: Critical user journeys from entry to completion.


**Scope**: Full page loads, navigation clicks, form submission paths, metadata availability.


**NOT testing**: detailed error messages, complex multi-step flows (defer until later).


**Location**: `e2e/` folder with `.spec.ts` extension.


**Examples**:
- User lands on home, sees CTA
- User clicks About link, loads /about without 404
- Robots.txt is available and valid
- Sitemap includes expected routes

## Safe workflow for adding tests (how Codex should proceed)


1. **Choose surface**: Pick one component or page from the testing-matrix.md "Now" list
2. **Create test file**: Add `.test.tsx` collocated with the component or under `e2e/` for E2E
3. **Write minimum assertions**: Use the testing matrix "minimum assertion" as the assertion target
4. **Run the test**: Verify it passes against the current code
5. **Commit and verify**: Ensure lint, type-check, and build still pass
6. **Repeat**: Move to next surface; do not batch multiple test files at once
7. **Check coverage**: Once all smoke tests are in, review coverage to identify any obvious gaps


### Example safe workflow


```bash
# 1. Create unit test for page-placeholder component
# (edit: src/components/sections/page-placeholder.test.tsx)


# 2. Run and verify
npm run test -- src/components/sections/page-placeholder.test.tsx


# 3. Check lint and type
npm run lint
npm run test:type


# 4. Commit
git add src/components/sections/page-placeholder.*
git commit -m "test: add unit test for page-placeholder"


# 5. Move to next surface (e.g., site-header)
# (repeat pattern)
```


## Tips for test maintainability


1. **Keep tests small and focused**: one test = one behavior
2. **Use descriptive names**: describe what the user should see/experience, not implementation
3. **Avoid implementation details**: test role/text/visual output, not component state/props directly
4. **Use fixtures for shared data**: store nav routes, metadata templates in `src/test/fixtures/`
5. **Comment WHY, not WHAT**: if the assertion is not obvious, explain the business reason
6. **Defer advanced mocking**: keep mocking minimal; mock only external APIs, not internal modules
7. **Run full test suite before committing**: ensure no regressions
